# Customer tenant self-service

Issue #176 completes the existing tenant-provisioning and invitation flows with customer member management and workspace selection.

Organization Admins open **Access administration** to filter and sort accepted identities by identity, workspace or role. Accepted invitation email is the display label where available; pre-existing identities retain their IdP subject. Invitations remain a separate list with pending/accepted/revoked/expired status. Each active workspace role has **Manage role**, allowing replacement or immediate removal with a required reason and confirmation. Organization Admin grants require a separate explicit confirmation. Self-edits are refused; another Organization Admin must manage the acting user's access. The existing **Deactivate everywhere** flow remains the tenant-wide offboarding action.

`POST /api/v1/access/members/role` accepts `userId`, `workspaceId`, `expectedRole`, `roleName` (null removes that role), `reason`, and `confirmTenantAdmin`. It rechecks the actor's tenant-admin membership, locks the target identity/membership, rejects stale selections, preserves the prior role's expiry and all unrelated memberships, and commits its audit event atomically. Removing the last effective workspace role expires that workspace's resource entitlements. A transport without transaction support fails closed. The response contains the audit event ID; the broader customer audit browser remains tracked under #181/C9.

The sidebar shows the authenticated subject and real workspace/tenant names. Users with multiple active memberships in their current tenant receive a native keyboard-operable workspace selector. Selection is verified against `/api/v1/me` before it is persisted. Signed OIDC/SAML broker sessions may select another workspace within their authenticated tenant; Postgres re-resolves all permissions and entitlements. Service identities cannot change their signed workspace context. Direct OIDC continues to validate selectors and membership through the same authorization repository. This does not introduce cross-tenant discovery or let a browser mutate signed tenant identity.

A successful switch reloads the document, discarding loaded data, modals, selections, searches, analytics and capabilities before the next workspace renders. All customer API adapters—including analytics, exports and uploads—send the selected context. Each loaded page pins its context so an outstanding upload cannot change destination when another tab updates the preference. Other tabs reload on a selection change. Review preferences and resumable upload records are namespaced by workspace. If a saved selection is no longer authorized, **Reset workspace selection** clears it and retries the authenticated default context.

The second demo workspace intentionally has no data and read-only document capabilities, making stale data and permission reuse observable in regression tests. The accessibility surface matrix includes access administration; additional matrix tests cover the selector, role confirmation, focus restoration and reload isolation. Real IdP/Postgres/browser integration and two-tenant evidence still belong to the production-like UAT gates; this implementation does not satisfy those live-evidence gates by itself.

## Hardening notes (SCIM, bulk invite, lifecycle)

- Source-connection governance (`source_connection.create|test|pause|resume|reauthorize|revoke|token_refresh|oauth_start|oauth_declined`, target type `source_connection`; an OAuth start or decline for a connection that does not exist yet targets the provider key) writes its audit event in the same transaction as the change, and the tenant access audit listing and its CSV export (`GET /api/v1/access/audit`) include those events. Metadata holds only the provider key or error class, never a credential. This was previously missing from the listing's action filter (#253).
- SCIM requests are rate limited per client address and per targeted tenant before any database lookup (429 with `Retry-After`); the limits are process-local per instance. Minting or rotating the SCIM token writes an `access.scim.configured` audit event in the same transaction (it records the previous settings, never the token) and appears in the tenant access audit. SCIM user creation provisions the identity and the SCIM record atomically, and unexpected SCIM 500s log the error class.
- `POST /api/v1/access/invitations/bulk` no longer confirms `tenant_admin` rows implicitly: those rows fail with `tenant_admin_confirmation_required` unless the request carries `?confirmTenantAdmin=true`. Per-row failures report a stable code, not the raw error message.
- `POST /api/v1/admin/identity-lifecycle` refuses to disable or demote the calling administrator (`cannot_deactivate_current_user`, `cannot_change_current_user`) or the last active tenant administrator (`last_tenant_admin`), all 409. SQL business errors raised by the identity-lifecycle, access-policy and support-access functions map to 4xx instead of 500.
- Upload access remains uploader-or-`admin` (`lib/server/upload-access.ts`). Open product decision: an `accountadmin` of another workspace in the tenant maps to `admin` and can act on any tenant upload.

## Data retention and full data export (F10)

The same page (`/access-self-service`) now shows, for Organization Admins only:

- **Data retention and legal holds**: a read-only table of how long each class of the organization's data is kept, what happens when the contract ends, and any legal hold (matter reference and what it covers). Corvis operations set and lift both; a hold overrides retention. The view hides who placed a hold.
- **Full data export**: an Organization Admin states why the export is needed; a *different* Organization Admin approves (or rejects with a reason) before anything is built, and the requester can withdraw it until the build starts. When built, the export is a checksummed archive (published data, the access audit trail, a source-document inventory and the source document files themselves) behind a single-use, ten-minute download link. Only data the organization may redistribute under its contracts is included, source files only where it also holds source-file access, and the manifest says how many funds, documents and source files were left out. A large data set is split into numbered files, all listed with their checksums in the manifest, and while the export is being built the request shows a size estimate and its progress. Every step is audited and appears in the access audit trail.

Demo mode serves both from in-memory stores (a colleague's pending request is seeded so the approval flow can be exercised; the archive is built at the moment of approval from the demo catalog). Contract: `API_CONVENTIONS.md`, "Data retention and full data export (F10)".

## Sign-in and session policy (F7)

The same page now has a **Sign-in and session policy** section for Organization Admins (story F7, #263), shipped in slices. What is in place:

- **Read-only identity view**: the identity provider (when Corvis operations recorded one for the organization: issuer or entity id, audience, status and whether token binding is enforced; otherwise the single shared provider's issuer), the organization's **verified email domains**, SCIM status (enabled, default workspace and role, active users) and how many users sign in by each method. Initial identity-provider, domain and SCIM setup stays Corvis-assisted (#78), so nothing here can change it; Corvis operations record the provider and domains through `POST /api/v1/admin/tenant-identity` (operations tenant only, audited for the organization).
- **Session limits**: an idle timeout (15 minutes to 8 hours) and a maximum session length (1 hour to 7 days), each optional, within bounds Corvis defines and enforces in SQL. A session past a limit ends and the person signs in again at the identity provider.
- **Sign out everywhere** for a named person: ends every session Corvis has seen for them, effective on their next request. They keep their access and can sign in again; removing access remains the separate deactivate action.
- Every change is audited in the tenant access audit and every Organization Admin is sent a mandatory security notice by email. Demo mode serves the same section from an in-memory store (the people and their sessions are a fixed illustration, and nothing is enforced on demo requests because demo mode has no real sessions).

Contract and enforcement: `API_CONVENTIONS.md`, "Sign-in and session policy (F7)"; storage and SQL rules: `DATA_PLATFORM.md`, "Organization session policy (migration 087)".

### Assumptions this slice makes (decisions for Corvis)

These are the most conservative readings of an ambiguous story, not settled product decisions:

1. **A session is the identity provider's `sid` (or `jti`).** Idle time and length are measured by Corvis, from when it *first sees* the session and when it last saw a request, not from when the person signed in at the identity provider. A session Corvis has never seen cannot be listed or signed out. If the identity provider sends neither `sid` nor `jti` the verifier synthesises a per-token id that cannot be measured, so **while any limit is set those sessions are refused** (fail closed). Turn a limit on only for tenants whose identity provider issues a stable session id.
2. **An expired session is dead for good.** A session past a limit is never refreshed, so it stays refused until the policy is relaxed; the person must sign in again and the identity provider must issue a *new* `sid`. If the identity provider silently re-authenticates and reuses the same `sid`, the person stays locked out until their identity-provider session ends.
3. **"Sign out everywhere" ends sessions Corvis has seen, in Corvis only.** It does not call the identity provider, so the person's identity-provider session stays signed in (and they can sign straight back in). A session that has not yet made any request to Corvis cannot be revoked.
4. **No tenant lock-out.** A policy change never revokes anything by itself and its minimum values (15 minutes idle, 1 hour total) cannot lock an administrator out for longer than one sign-in; sign-out-everywhere refuses the caller themself; and a limit cannot be saved from a session that cannot be measured (`409 session_not_measurable`), which is the case that would otherwise refuse every session of the organization, the admin's own included. Clearing the limits is always allowed. Administrators are not exempt from the limits.
5. **One shared token verifier, with an optional per-tenant record (F7e, #338).** Corvis still verifies every bearer token against the one issuer and audience configured for the deployment (`CORVIS_AUTH_ISSUER`/`CORVIS_AUTH_AUDIENCE`). A per-tenant identity-provider record (protocol, issuer, audience, status, migration 095) is operator-managed and shown read-only. **Token binding is OFF unless an operator sets `enforceTokenBinding`** for the tenant (only for an active OIDC record). When on, an interactive request is authorized only if the verified token's issuer and audience equal the record; a mismatch, or a request with no verified token claims (a signed gateway assertion), is refused with the generic 401 and never recorded as a session (fail closed). Because there is one shared verifier today, binding is a consistency pin (a tenant pinned to an issuer refuses sessions if the platform's issuer or audience ever changes, or a second issuer is added); it does **not** yet select a different identity provider or signing keys per tenant. A record whose issuer differs from the deployment's issuer, with binding on, refuses that whole tenant, so an operator must check the values first. Binding does not apply to service accounts, SAML sessions or queued background re-authorization. No record, or binding off (the default): behavior is unchanged.
6. **Queued work is exempt from the idle and length limits.** An export or schedule a person started is re-authorized in the background without applying (or extending) their session limits, but it is still cancelled by sign-out-everywhere.

### Housekeeping and observability (F7d)

**Housekeeping.** `tenant_session_activity` has one row per session Corvis has seen. The private delivery tick (`/api/internal/delivery`, task `sessionActivitySweep`) deletes the rows of sessions **not seen for 90 days**, at most 5,000 per tick, through `corvis_control.purge_tenant_session_activity` (migration 091). It is safe by construction: the function refuses a retention shorter than 11,520 minutes, the longest allowed maximum session (10,080) plus a day, so a record that an idle or maximum-length limit could still be judging is never a candidate; an active session refreshes its record at least every 30 seconds so it is never one either; `session_revocation` is never touched, so a revoked or signed-out session stays refused whether or not its activity record exists; and sign-out-everywhere keeps working on every record that remains. The purge writes no audit event (the rows are bookkeeping) and logs how many it removed (`session_activity.purged`, with a `metric.count` of the same name). One consequence to know: a session that comes back after more than 90 days of silence is recorded as a new session and measured from then (and sign-out-everywhere could not have named it).

**Observability.** `lib/server/authorization.ts` emits, for every enforced check, the `auth.session_policy` `metric.duration` (the latency of `enforce_session_policy`, tagged `outcome` with the verdict: `ok`, `idle_timeout`, `max_session`, `untracked_session` or `unknown`), and for every denial an `auth.session_policy_denied` `metric.count` tagged `reason`, next to the existing `auth.session_policy_denied` warning. Service identities and background re-authorization are exempt and emit nothing. No subject or session id is ever logged. The denial rate is denials divided by checks, from the same two series.

**Alerts** (`infra/terraform/modules/gcp-observability/application-slo.tf`, thresholds in `ops/slos.yaml`; both start as WARNING / SEV3 and should be tuned from production):

| Alert | Fires when | What to look at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `session-policy-enforcement-p95` | p95 of `auth.session_policy` above 100 ms for 15 minutes | Every authorized request pays for this one Postgres call. Check pool waits, the size of `tenant_session_activity` and that the `sessionActivitySweep` task is not failing (`delivery.task_failed`). |
| `session-policy-denials` | More than 200 `auth.session_policy_denied` per 5 minutes for 10 minutes | Group by the `reason` label. `idle_timeout` / `max_session`: an organization's limits are tighter than how its people work (ask its Organization Admins to relax them; clearing is always allowed). `untracked_session`: the identity provider stopped sending a stable `sid`/`jti` while a limit is set, which refuses everyone in that organization. `unknown`: the check answered nothing and failed closed, so look at Postgres first. |

### Not yet built (follow-ups)

- **Require SSO** (acceptance criterion 2): there is no non-SSO sign-in path inside Corvis to block (sign-in happens at the identity provider, and Corvis receives no claim saying how the person authenticated), so a setting would have nothing to enforce. It needs a decision on the signal (an `amr`/`idp` claim, a per-tenant provider binding, or provider-side enforcement).
- **IdP-enforced MFA display** (criterion 2): Corvis has no MFA signal from the identity provider today.
- **Verified email domains** (criterion 1, F7b #335, migration 095): shipped as a Corvis-assisted record. Operations verify a domain for one tenant at a time (a second claim is refused; at most 20 per tenant; lower-case ASCII, no wildcards), and an Organization Admin sees them read-only. Enforcement is **OFF for a tenant with no verified domain**. Once it has at least one, creating a NEW invitation (`email_domain_not_verified`, 422) or a NEW SCIM user (`400 invalidValue`) requires an address on exactly one of the verified domains (subdomains are not implied). It is checked only at creation: existing people, sign-in and invitation acceptance are never affected, so removing a domain locks nobody out. It applies to Corvis operators' invitations too (add the domain first). Not built: self-service domain verification (DNS challenge) and domain-based auto-join; both need a product decision.
- **Session-expiry experience (F7c, #336)**: a session the organization's policy ended (idle timeout or maximum length) now answers `401 session_ended_by_policy` instead of `authentication_required`, and the shell banner and access page say so. The code is raised only after the token verified and the person's membership resolved, so it reveals nothing to anyone else (an unknown, revoked, disabled or foreign identity, and an unmeasurable session, keep the generic code); the idle/maximum reason stays in the log (`auth.session_policy_denied`, `api.session_ended_by_policy`). Raw `fetch` call sites that use `throwIfUnauthenticated` cannot read the response body and keep the generic copy; the shell learns the reason from the workspace port's calls.
- **Provider-side session revocation and back-channel logout**: out of scope here; they need identity-provider support (a `logout_token` endpoint and the provider's session list). Until then a sign-out or expiry ends the Corvis session only.
- **Per-tenant verification keys / multiple issuers** (the full form of #338): token verification still uses one issuer, so selecting the verifier by tenant needs a decision on how the tenant is known before the token is verified (today it is an untrusted request header) and a bounded, cached lookup that cannot be used for unauthenticated database load.


## Service accounts (F6)

The same page also lets Organization Admins create **service accounts** for their own systems and issue, rotate and revoke their API credentials. A service account is a non-human identity with one ordinary role (Review Analyst, Analyst or Viewer, never an administrator) in one workspace, under the same entitlements and data rights as a person. The list shows each account's role, workspace, creator, last use and expiry, and flags a credential (or account) within 14 days of expiring. The credential is shown once at creation and cannot be retrieved again (only a hash is stored); a rotation leaves the old credential working for a short overlap you choose (up to 24 hours), and revocation stops every credential immediately. **Extend expiry** moves an account's expiry later (up to 365 days from the day you extend it) and advances its review date with it, and every account has an **owner**, an active Organization Admin who answers for it (the creator first): **Change owner** hands it to another, and an account whose owner has been deactivated keeps working but is flagged as needing a new owner and is not extended until it has one. **Deactivate account** removes the account everywhere (identity, memberships, entitlements, credentials) and is final. Every action is audited and appears in the access audit trail. Creating an account grants no fund or document access: until you grant some, it sees nothing. Under **Data access** on each account you can **Grant data access** to a fund or document and **Remove** it again, each with a stated reason that is kept in the audit trail. You can only grant what your organization is licensed to see (it owns the fund or document and holds a client-visible data right for it); anything else is refused, and if the organization's right later lapses the account's access ends with it and the screen flags the entry. Granting is read-only and for service accounts only; people's entitlements are still granted by Corvis operations. When an account or its credential is within 14 days of expiring, and again within 3, every Organization Admin gets a short email that cannot be turned off (it says that something expires, not which account; the page flags it). **Credentials are not yet accepted by the API**; the screen says so, and the open decision is in `SERVICE_ACCOUNTS.md`. Demo mode serves the section from an in-memory store seeded per demo tenant.
